**Jobsite coordination — explained for a beginner**
**Goal:** Leave knowing what *Jobsite coordination* means in Construction Management, the main steps, and one mistake to avoid.
You asked about: Explain jobsite coordination like I'm new — who talks to who and why it matters.
**What Construction Management is**
Construction management coordinates people, materials, schedule, safety, and quality so the building comes together in the right order.
**What “Jobsite coordination” means here**
“Planning the next few days” means a next-few-days plan: which trades, which areas, which inspections, which deliveries. Without it, electricians show up before the building is weather-tight, or inspections get called on unfinished rooms.


**Safety first:** If a person could get hurt, stop. Fix that before speed or the schedule.

**Steps good people follow**
1. Know today’s work face and what trade is supposed to be there.
2. Walk the site; compare plan to reality.
3. Remove blockers: missing info, unsafe conditions, wrong material.
4. Communicate holds and releases in writing short form.
5. Update the next-few-days plan so tomorrow is not a surprise.
**Example of a clear message**
You → Site team: First-stage electrical inspection moved from Thursday to Monday. Room 204 fire-seal material in the openings is incomplete (photo). Electrician notified. That room loses one day; other rooms stay on plan.
**Example of a weak message (avoid)**
Inspector is here — figure it out.
**Most common beginner mistake: Calling an inspection or a trade before the area is actually ready**
New coordinators book the inspector to “keep schedule” when fire-seal work or required tests are incomplete. Everyone waits, the inspection fails, and trust drops.
**What careful people do instead**
- Walk the area with a checklist before you call the inspector or the next trade.
- Write what “ready” means for that inspection type.
- If not ready, rebook early — do not hope.
- Tell trades the truth about the date so they can replan labor.
