**Finding leaks — explained for a beginner**
**Goal:** Leave knowing what *Finding leaks* means in Plumbing, the main steps, and one mistake to avoid.
You asked about: What does finding leaks actually mean day one — plain English.
**What Plumbing is**
Plumbing brings clean water in and takes dirty water out through supply pipes, drains, and vents.
**What “Finding leaks” means here**
“Drain and vent pipes” are the system that carries wastewater to the sewer or septic and lets air into the system so water can flow smoothly. Without a proper vent, drains gurgle, traps lose water, and sewer gas can enter the building. On site you are always asking: does water flow downhill, and can air get in where it should?


**Safety first:** If a person could get hurt, stop. Fix that before speed or the schedule.

**Steps good people follow**
1. Know whether you are on supply (pressurized clean water) or drain/waste/vent (gravity and air).
2. Dry-fit or mark the run before you glue, solder, or press.
3. Keep slope on drains so waste flows; keep vents open so traps do not siphon.
4. Support pipe, protect it through studs, and leave access where code needs it.
5. Pressure-test or water-test before walls close.
**Example of a clear message**
You → Crew lead: Second-floor bath shower drain does not slope enough on the long run (only about 1/16 inch drop per foot). I stopped before gluing the joints. Photo of the level attached. Need a new pipe path through the wall, or a re-route.
**Example of a weak message (avoid)**
Chat: drains are in, covered them up, vent “should be fine.”
**Most common beginner mistake: Skipping slope and vent checks because the wall is about to close**
New plumbers rush pipe joint gluings when drywall is scheduled. A drain without enough slope, or a vent left capped, may pass a quick look and fail later as chronic clogs or smell complaints — after the wall is finished.
**What careful people do instead**
- Check slope with a level before you glue or cement the joint.
- Trace the vent path to open air (or an approved air admittance setup where allowed).
- Test before cover: water or air test per the job rules.
- If the how a story is angled blocks the vent route, stop and get a revised path — do not “make it fit” silently.
