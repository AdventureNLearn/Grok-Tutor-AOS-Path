**First 90 days on the job — explained for a beginner**
**Goal:** Leave knowing what *First 90 days on the job* means in Public Information Literacy, the main steps, and one mistake to avoid.
You asked about: I don't know the words yet — start from zero on how careful reading of claims works.
**What Public Information Literacy is**
Public information literacy is learning to check claims about shared decisions by reading original documents carefully — and labeling what you still do not know.
**What “First 90 days on the job” means here**
“How approvals work” in this tutor means the general idea of apply → review → conditions → check. We teach habits, not place-specific rules. Beginners argue from social posts instead of the original file.
**Steps good people follow**
1. Write the question in plain words
2. Find the original document or recording when you can
3. Note who wrote it and when
4. Separate what the document says from what people claim online
5. List who can decide next, if that is public
6. Write sources so someone else can re-check
**Example of a clear message**
Summary: The posted conditions PDF (retrieved today) lists quarterly monitoring in condition 12. I am not adding claims beyond that text.
**Example of a weak message (avoid)**
Someone online said it is illegal; must be true.
**Most common beginner mistake: Treating a viral post as equal to the original document**
Rumors move faster than PDFs. You mislead yourself and others if you never open the original source.
**What careful people do instead**
- Ask for the document name or number if one exists
- Read the actual conditions or minutes
- Note dates
- Cite what you read
- Update your notes when the file changes
